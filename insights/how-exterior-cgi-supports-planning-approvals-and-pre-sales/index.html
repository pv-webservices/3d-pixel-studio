How Exterior CGI Supports Planning Approvals and Pre-Sales

Showing a building in its real context

Exterior CGI is often treated as launch marketing—something commissioned once the design is settled. Built directly from the architectural drawings, it does far more: it is the clearest way to show how a project will actually sit on its site.

Elevations and site plans describe a building accurately, but they describe it in isolation. Decision-makers—planning officers, investors, landowners and future residents—need to understand height, massing and setbacks against the streets, neighbouring structures and skyline around them. A correctly scaled exterior view answers those questions in a single frame.

That is why our exterior work starts with geometry, not atmosphere. The façade, floor-to-floor heights, balcony projections and podium levels are modelled from the drawing set and checked against the site before any lighting or landscaping is added.

Why planning reviews move faster with accurate views

Approval discussions slow down when reviewers have to interpret drawings on their own. Questions about overlooking, street frontage, the relationship to adjacent plots or the visual weight of a tower are difficult to settle from orthographic sheets alone.

Views taken from real eye-level positions—the main approach road, the nearest junction, a neighbouring building—let every stakeholder see the same thing. Because each camera is set up from the site survey rather than chosen for drama, the images can be trusted as a representation of the proposal, not a flattering version of it.

An exterior view earns its place in an approval set when it is accurate enough to be challenged—and still holds up.

Day, dusk and night: one model, several decisions

Once the exterior model is built, it becomes a reusable asset. The same scene can be rendered in daylight to review materials and shadow, at dusk to show the building as most people will remember it, and at night to test façade lighting and signage before anything is specified.

For masterplans and townships, aerial views clarify phasing, internal roads, amenity placement and open space—information that is hard to read from a layout plan but immediately understood from above. Each view serves a different conversation, and all of them come from the same verified geometry.

From approval set to pre-sales campaign

The visuals that help a project through review are the same visuals that sell it. Hoardings, brochures, site-office displays and digital campaigns all need hero images that are both striking and defensible—buyers increasingly compare what they were shown with what is delivered.

Starting from an accurate model means marketing images can be produced without reinterpreting the design. Revisions made during approvals carry straight through to the final campaign set, so the project is presented consistently from the first meeting to the launch.

Conclusion

Exterior CGI is most valuable when it is treated as part of the design process rather than an afterthought. Grounded in drawings and set in real context, it supports clearer approvals, confident stakeholder decisions and pre-sales material that reflects what will actually be built.
